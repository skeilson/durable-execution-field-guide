# 6.1 Crash mid-order

**Time:** 30 minutes · **Run:** `npm run m6 -- 6.1` · **Builds on:** 3.3, 3.11

## The ticket

> **From:** Dana Reyes, Platform Lead, Northwind Outfitters · **Priority:** High
> **Subject:** Order service crashed mid-checkout — are orders lost??
>
> Our order service ran out of memory and was down for a few minutes before Kubernetes restarted it. We had checkouts in flight. Finance wants to know if customers were charged without their orders shipping, and whether we need to re-run anything. Example order: order-1001.

## Investigation

The order was submitted with its order id as the idempotency key, so it's easy to find:

```bash
npx restate sql "SELECT id, target, created_at FROM sys_invocation WHERE idempotency_key = 'order-1001'"
npx restate invocations describe <id>
```

While the service was down:

```
Status:   backing-off  (Retried 4 time(s). Next retry in 357 ms.)
Error:    [500] error when calling 'http://localhost:9080/':
          h2 pool connection error: Connection refused (os error 111)
Journal:
 [1]–[2]  Run reserve inventory      ✔
 [3]–[5]  Call Payments/charge       ✔
 [6]–[7]  OneWayCall Emails/send     ✔
 [8]–[9]  Sleep wait for pickup      ✔ fired
 >> backing-off
```

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `backing-off` + `Connection refused` | Restate is retrying because it can't reach the service. The order logic is fine. |
| Charge call has its notification (entry 5) | The charge completed and is recorded; it won't run again |
| No `ship order` entry yet | Shipping hasn't happened; it will after recovery |

## Root cause and resolution

- **Root cause:** the service process died (out of memory). Restate kept each order's journal and retried with backoff.
- **Resolution:** once the service is healthy, in-flight orders resume from their last recorded step. `restate invocations resume` skips the remaining backoff.
- **Impact:** delay only. No lost orders, no duplicate charges, **provided** no external call was interrupted mid-flight without an idempotency key (see 6.2).

## Runbook entry

```
SYMPTOM   Orders stalled after a service outage, crash or deploy.
CHECK     restate invocations list                → backing-off entries?
          restate invocations describe <id>       → read Error + journal
LIKELY    "Connection refused"/5xx to the service URL → service down or unreachable
FIX       Restore the service. Optional: restate invocations resume <target or id>
VERIFY    Invocations complete; system of record shows one side effect per step
ESCALATE  Error comes from inside the handler (stack trace), not the connection;
          or the journal shows a step that keeps re-running
```

## In Temporal

A dead worker doesn't produce errors in Temporal: workflows stay Running with pending tasks waiting on the task queue, and `temporal task-queue describe` shows no pollers (lesson 4.4). Recovery is the same: bring workers back and workflows resume from their history.

## Reply to the customer

> Hi Dana,
>
> Short version: **no orders were lost, nothing needs to be re-run, and order-1001 was charged once and has shipped.**
>
> When your service went down, Restate kept a record of each in-flight order's progress. For order-1001, that record shows inventory was reserved and the card was charged *before* the crash, and the order was waiting for warehouse pickup. While the service was unavailable, Restate retried with increasing delays (you'll see `backing-off` with "connection refused" for that window). Once Kubernetes restarted the service, the order resumed where it stopped. The charge step was not repeated.
>
> To check the other orders from that window:
>
> ```
> restate invocations list               # anything still backing off?
> restate invocations describe <id>      # status and step-by-step progress
> ```
>
> Anything still backing off will resume on its next retry, or immediately with `restate invocations resume Orders/checkout`.
>
> One thing worth checking on your side: if the service died *during* a call to your payment provider (not between steps), that call will be retried. That's safe if you send the provider an idempotency key. Do you? If not, I'm happy to walk through it. It's a small change.
>
> As a final check, I'd reconcile the outage window against your payment provider's records, since that's your source of truth.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]
