# 8.3 Writing replies customers trust

**Time:** 30 minutes

Look back at the replies in Module 6. They all follow the same shape.

## The shape

1. **Answer the scary question first.** "No orders were lost." "Nothing was charged twice except bc-7731."
2. **Explain what happened in their terms**, in a few sentences. Their order, their service, their deploy. Not the product's internals.
3. **Give the fix or workaround**, concretely: a command, a code snippet, a link.
4. **Show how to verify it** themselves.
5. **Flag the adjacent risk** they didn't ask about ("do you send idempotency keys?").
6. **Offer the next step**, if there is one.

## Before and after

**Before:**

> Hi, this is expected behavior. Restate provides at-least-once execution for side effects inside ctx.run. You should use idempotency keys. See the docs.

Technically true, but it reads as dismissive, gives no evidence, and doesn't tell them what to do.

**After:**

> Hi Marco, thanks for the order id. I've traced bc-7731 end to end. Restate ran checkout once, but the charge step's first attempt timed out after your provider had already charged, and the retry charged again because the provider couldn't recognize it as a repeat. The fix is one line: pass a stable idempotency key like `${orderId}/charge`… I'd also suggest checking your provider for other duplicate pairs; happy to help with that query.

## Tone rules

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Use their name and their identifiers | Use templates that ignore their details |
| Say what you checked | Say "this is expected behavior" without evidence |
| Own platform problems plainly | Blame the customer, even when it's their code |
| Explain *why* when it helps them avoid it next time | Lecture |
| Give dates or next update times for open issues | Leave them guessing |
| Admit what you don't know yet | Guess confidently |

## When it's your bug

> You're right, this is a bug in Restate, and I'm sorry for the disruption. Here's what we know: … Workaround until the fix ships: … I'll update you by 3pm Mountain tomorrow, sooner if we have a fix.

Short, honest, with a workaround and a time for the next update.
