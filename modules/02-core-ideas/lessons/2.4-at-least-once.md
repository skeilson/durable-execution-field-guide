# 2.4 At-least-once: the gap

**Time:** 15 minutes · **Engine:** [`v2-replay.ts`](../engine/v2-replay.ts) (unchanged)

## The window

Look closely at when v2 writes to the journal:

```ts
const result = await fn();     // 1. the bank charges the card
                               // ← 💥 what if we crash HERE?
this.journal.entries.push(…);  // 2. we record that it happened
```

Between "the side effect happened" and "we recorded it" there is a window. It might be a millisecond, but at a million orders a day it **will** be hit.

If the process dies in that window, the journal has no record of the charge. On restart, the engine reaches the charge step, finds nothing at that position, and executes it again.

## No engine can close it alone

You might think a cleverer engine could fix this: record "about to charge" first, then charge, then record "charged." But after a crash between those, all the engine knows is "I was about to charge." Did the charge go through? That's the **ambiguous failure** from lesson 1.3 again. The engine's view of the world stops at its own process.

So every durable execution engine, including Restate and Temporal, makes this promise:

> **Each step's work runs at least once. Once a step's result is recorded, it's never re-executed.**

That's called **at-least-once execution**. The honest summary is "almost always once, occasionally twice, never zero times."

## Why at-least-once is still a huge improvement

| | Without durable execution | With durable execution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crash **between** steps | Rerun repeats every finished step | Finished steps are replayed, never repeated |
| Crash **inside** a step | Rerun repeats every finished step | Only the one interrupted step repeats |
| Workflow forgotten after a crash | Common: someone has to notice | Never: the engine resumes it |

The engine shrinks the danger from "everything might repeat" to "the one step that was in flight might repeat." Lesson 2.5 makes that one repeat harmless.

> **🎧 Support angle**
> "But you guarantee exactly-once!" is a common customer misunderstanding, and marketing pages for this category don't always help. The precise statement: the *workflow* makes progress exactly once (each step's result is recorded once and reused), but each step's *side effect* is at-least-once. Explaining this clearly and kindly, then showing how idempotency closes the gap, is a core support skill.
